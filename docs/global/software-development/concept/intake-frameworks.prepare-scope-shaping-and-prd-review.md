# Intake Frameworks — Concept

## What

Named frameworks are optional priming references. Preparation begins with the
work and its open questions; when one of those questions exposes a reasoning
shape that an established framework handles well, the agent may invoke that
framework by its canonical name.

The framework follows the situation. It never precedes the situation as a
menu, a default stack, or a list whose traversal stands in for judgment. “Apply
Impact Mapping” can activate useful trained structure when actor-to-outcome
relationships are genuinely complex. The same phrase is constraining ceremony
when the work does not earn that structure.

A framework name is a salience cue, not evidence and not authority. Its output
must still answer the preparation questions, explain why it matters here, and
rest on an inspectable premise or a named assumption. Frameworks may expand
the candidate set; they do not decide product intent, scope, or correctness.

Procedures therefore describe the question to answer, not a catalogue of tools
to run. They may name a framework at the point where it is useful, but they do
not prescribe a default sequence or require an explicit not-applicable result
for tools that were never needed.

## Why

Well-known framework names can activate more domain structure across models
than a freshly invented procedural description. That value disappears when a
menu narrows attention to the listed methods, rewards visible framework use,
or makes the artifact prove that a checklist was traversed. Situational
invocation preserves the priming benefit while leaving discovery open to the
domain intelligence already present in the model.
