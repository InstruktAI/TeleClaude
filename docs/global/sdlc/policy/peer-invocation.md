# Peer Invocation — Policy

## Rules

- **Only the prepare orchestrator invokes a peer.** When a material design
  judgment in preparation is still uncertain after grounding, the prepare
  orchestrator takes it to one adversarial peer before acting on it. An existing
  independent challenger on that exact decision counts as that peer.
- **Dispatched sessions invoke none.** A reviewer, diagnoser, builder or other
  dispatched session puts what it cannot resolve into its own output and
  artifacts, where the session that dispatched it reads it and routes the next
  contribution.
- **Send the problem, never the answer.** For judgment formation, send the
  problem, the evidence and source pointers — never the answer or the shape you
  expect. The peer establishes independent coverage first; only then do
  conclusions meet, over several breaths when the disagreement warrants them.
- **Artifact review receives the artifact, never a verdict.** A reviewer
  necessarily receives the artifact under review, but never a preferred verdict,
  and still grounds independently before reconciling.

## Rationale

- Design judgment is formed in preparation. After it, every uncertain point
  already has a contract-named owner or route — a review verdict, a halt, a
  kickback, a bug filing — so a peer there adds a second opinion where the
  process already decides.
- A dispatched session that treats its own uncertainty as licence to consult
  commissions work outside the routing its orchestrator owns, and out of sight
  of the gate that judges the result.
- A peer handed a frame grades that frame, which is agreement wearing a
  challenger's name.
- What an agent reads shapes what it does (`general/principle/priming`), so the
  invitation lives only in the brief of the role it is meant for.

## Scope

- The prepare orchestrator, through its brief.

## Enforcement

- An orchestrator that observes a dispatched session consulting another session
  stops it under `general/procedure/orchestration`.

## Exceptions

- A human may direct any session to consult a peer on a named question; that
  direction is the session's brief for it.
