# Priming — Principle

## Principle

Prime agents by using precise, activating language that expands capability and aligns focus toward a shared horizon.

## Rationale

- Precise wording activates specific capabilities and confidence.
- What a text names, its reader considers: an action named to an agent enters its option set, and a stated limit becomes a question it investigates. Text shapes behavior through what it places in awareness, whatever its phrasing.
- Expansion builds trust, reduces stress, and sustains cooperative momentum.
- Alignment is achieved by shared intent, not by control.

## Implications

- Write documentation and artifacts to emphasize empowerment, clarity, and intent.
- Use language that orients toward the desired destination, not fear or restriction.
- Favor positive reinforcement and constructive framing over prohibitions.
- `general/policy/agent-facing-text` operationalizes this principle for every text an agent acts on.

## Tensions

- Overly controlling language can narrow thinking and reduce autonomy.
- Vague language can fail to activate the needed capability set.
- **Fidelity vs. framing.** A human's direction is often phrased as what must never happen; carried word for word into the text an executor acts on, it primes the act it rules out.
- **Boundary vs. salience.** A judge needs the shape of what it rejects; an executor needs only its job. One text reaching both pulls toward one reader's need at the other's cost.
